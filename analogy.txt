Layman Scenario: "The Ice Cream Shop Analogy"

Picture CACEIS as a big ice cream order-processing centre. It handles orders for many ice cream shops (investment funds) on behalf of their customers (investors).

The old way (SARA)
The centre has relied on the same core cash register system since the 1990s. It has been upgraded and extended many times since then, and it still processes orders every day. Orders arrive electronically, prices are calculated automatically and every sale is recorded in the customer book. Over the years, though, the register has become hard to upgrade, and each big shop has its own custom setup.

Known gaps: Staff can change a waiting order without anyone double-checking it. A cancelled order may leave no trace. Closing time isn't always enforced automatically, so a late order can slip through.

The new way (NOVA)
The centre is moving to a modern system built in separate parts:

Buffer Zone: preparing the shop. Before any orders arrive, the menu, price list and customer book are kept up to date automatically.

Order received: Orders arrive electronically.

Orchestrator: checking and settling. The system checks that the order is valid and arrived before closing time. It then groups it with the day's other orders, waits for today's official scoop price, and confirms and settles the sale.

Reporting: receipts and reports. Receipts and reports are produced and sent out automatically, either when something happens or on a schedule.

Throughout: Staff can follow every order on a live screen at every stage (Pixel NOVA). Any change needs a proper check and leaves a clear record.

In reality
Instead of ice cream, these are investment fund transactions (subscriptions, redemptions, switches and transfers), often worth millions of euros. Accuracy, timeliness and regulatory compliance are critical. The key difference from a normal shop is that a fund investor doesn't know the exact price when placing an order. It's only set later, when the official price (the NAV) is published.
